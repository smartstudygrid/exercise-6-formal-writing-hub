The 3 Exercise 6 Text Types at a Glance

Type 1

Article / Essay / Argumentative

Discuss controversial or open topics (e.g., Should mobile phones be allowed in schools?, Should zoos be shut down?, Should animal testing for medicine be banned?). Presents balanced arguments with a clear stance.

Focus: Thesis, PEEL, Ethical & Policy Arguments
Type 2

Formal Report

Presents factual findings about events, trips, or facilities (e.g., School trip to a museum / amusement park, Science Exhibition, Health & Fitness Week, Facility audits). Uses subheadings and passive voice.

Focus: Subheadings, Observations, Actionable Recommendations
Type 3

Product / Venue / Media Review

Evaluates an experience or product for peers (e.g., Mobile apps, tech gadgets, new coffee shops/restaurants, amusement parks, films, books). Details pros, cons, ambiance, and recommendations.

Focus: Features, Performance, Rating & Verdict

Golden Rules for Success

  • Use a Captivating Title: Write 3 to 4 words, preferably in ALL CAPS or underlined (e.g., WHAT MAKES A FILM ENJOYABLE?).
  • Structure into 4 Clear Paragraphs: Introduction, 2 main body paragraphs (arguments/findings), and a formal conclusion.
  • Maintain Formal Register: Avoid informal slang, emojis, or contractions (write do not, cannot, should not instead of don't, can't).
  • Use Sophisticated Connectors: Employ formal linking devices like Furthermore, On the other hand, Consequently, To conclude.

Major Pitfalls to Avoid

  • Using Informal Conversational Tone: Don't treat Exercise 6 like Exercise 5. Avoid informal greetings like "Hi guys!" or informal slang.
  • Simply Copying Prompt Comments: The prompt comments are starting points. Always rephrase them in your own words with added development.
  • One-Sided Rants without Counter-arguments: In essays, missing a counter-argument weakens the evaluation and caps Content marks.
  • Giving Away Movie/Book Endings: In reviews, spoiling the ending ruins the recommendation aspect for the audience.

Exercise 6 Writing Formulas & Structures

Title & Opening Hook Approx. 25–35 Words

1st Paragraph: Catchy Title + Hook + Thesis

Hooks the reader with a relevant question or bold assertion regarding school policy, animal welfare, or technology.

• Example 1 (Mobile Phones in School): "SHOULD SMARTPHONES BE BANNED IN CLASSROOMS? Is constant connectivity empowering students or destroying academic focus?"

• Example 2 (Closing Zoos): "ARE ZOOS PRISONS OR SANCTUARIES? Should wild animals be kept in captivity for human entertainment?"

• Example 3 (Medical Animal Testing): "THE ETHICS OF ANIMAL TESTING: Does saving human lives justify animal suffering?"

PEEL Body 1 Approx. 50–55 Words

2nd Paragraph: Core Supporting Arguments

Develop major reasons supporting your main thesis with logical evidence and impact analysis.

• Mobile Phones: "Smartphones provide instantaneous access to educational research and digital textbooks during group projects."

• Zoo Closure: "Modern zoological parks play a critical role in breeding endangered species and raising conservation awareness."

• Animal Testing: "Medical research on animal models has led to life-saving breakthroughs in vaccines and cancer therapies."

PEEL Body 2 Approx. 45–50 Words

3rd Paragraph: Counter-Argument & Refutation

Acknowledge the opposing perspective fairly before refuting it with superior evidence.

• Counter: "Opponents argue that phones cause distraction or animal testing causes unnecessary pain."

• Refutation: "However, strict classroom regulations or synthetic testing alternatives effectively mitigate these concerns."

Conclusion Approx. 25–30 Words

4th Paragraph: Synthesis & Call to Action

Summarize key points and present a balanced final solution.

• "To conclude, rather than imposing outright bans, establishing clear guidelines ensures progress while addressing ethical risks."

Header & Purpose

REPORT TITLE & OBJECTIVE

State the objective clearly in formal, passive language across any event, trip, or facility topic.

• Museum Field Trip: "REPORT ON EDUCATIONAL FIELD TRIP TO THE NATIONAL MUSEUM"

• Science Exhibition: "REPORT ON THE ANNUAL SCIENCE & INNOVATION EXHIBITION"

• Health & Fitness Week: "REPORT ON SCHOOL HEALTH AND FITNESS WEEK ACTIVITIES"

Subheadings & Key Observations

FINDINGS & EVALUATION

Use section subheadings to systematically record student engagement and operational observations.

• Interactive Displays & Student Engagement: "It was observed that interactive robotics workshops at the science exhibition attracted over 200 participants."

• Logistics and Scheduling: "Although transport to the museum was well-organized, crowded exhibition halls reduced guided tour duration."

• Participation in Sports Workshops: "Over 80% of students actively participated in healthy cooking seminars and fitness challenges."

Recommendations

RECOMMENDATIONS & FUTURE IMPROVEMENTS

Provide practical, actionable recommendations for school leadership or event committees.

• "In order to maximize future educational benefits, it is recommended to extend the exhibition duration to two days and allocate smaller tour groups."

Catchy Opening

1. Hook & Introduction across Media, Venues, & Apps

Introduce the subject (App, Product, Coffee Shop, Amusement Park, Movie, or Book) with target audience hooks.

• Mobile App Review: "DUOLINGO APP: Is this mobile app truly the ultimate tool for mastering languages on the go?"

• Coffee Shop / Restaurant: "THE DAILY GRIND CAFÉ: Looking for the perfect study spot with artisanal coffee and warm ambiance?"

• Amusement Park: "ADRENALINE WORLD: Does this newly opened theme park deliver heart-pounding rollercoasters and fun?"

• Tech Product / Gadget: "SOUNDPULSE WIRELESS EARBUDS: Premium noise cancellation at a student-friendly price?"

Features & Analysis

2. In-Depth Features & Evaluation (Pros & Cons)

Analyze functionality, user experience, culinary quality, or ride excitement depending on subject.

• App/Tech: "The user interface is intuitive and fast. However, occasional software glitches disrupt offline access."

• Coffee Shop/Restaurant: "The espresso blends are exceptionally smooth and fresh. Nevertheless, weekend queue times can be tedious."

• Amusement Park: "The flagship hyper-coaster provides unmatched thrills, though ticket prices remain somewhat steep."

Final Verdict

3. Verdict, Target Audience & Star Rating

Conclude with clear recommendations regarding who should visit or purchase.

• "Overall rating: 4.5/5 Stars! Highly recommended for students, tech enthusiasts, or families looking for a weekend outing."
